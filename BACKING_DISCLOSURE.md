## 1. Purpose of This Disclosure

This document explains the current reserve and asset-backing status of Master Sovereign Token (MST).

It distinguishes between:

- Numerical values recorded in smart contract code.
- Project-declared figures or assumptions.
- Actual assets supported by documentary evidence.
- Assets whose existence, ownership, valuation, and availability have been independently verified.

These categories are not interchangeable.

The purpose of this disclosure is to prevent any nominal figure, technical parameter, project statement, or future intention from being misunderstood as proof of existing financial reserves.

## 2. Current Reserve Status

As of the date of this disclosure, MST does not claim independently verified financial reserves or independently verified asset backing.

No bank deposit, cash balance, commodity holding, property ownership, financial instrument, or other reserve asset is represented in this document as independently confirmed backing for MST.

The following distinctions are important:

| Item | Current Disclosure |
|---|---|
| Independently verified reserves | Not established |
| Independently verified collateral | Not established |
| Independent asset ownership confirmation | Not established |
| Independent reserve valuation | Not established |
| Confirmed redemption arrangement | Not established |
| Guaranteed token value | Not offered |
| Guaranteed minimum market price | Not offered |

This statement does not determine whether any person or entity may hold assets outside the scope of this disclosure. It means that such assets have not been established as verified MST reserves in this public documentation.

## 3. Nominal Smart Contract Value

The MST smart contract contains a numerical constant identified as:

`NOMINAL_BACKING_VALUATION`

The source-code expression is:

`500000000000 * 10**18`

This expression represents a nominal numerical value encoded in the smart contract.

It is not evidence of:

- 500 billion US dollars in reserves.
- A bank deposit or cash balance.
- Ownership of financial or physical assets.
- Verified collateral or security.
- A legally enforceable redemption obligation.
- A guaranteed market capitalization.
- A guaranteed token price.
- A fixed maximum token supply.
- Independent financial or accounting verification.

The presence of this constant in the contract does not create or prove an underlying financial asset.

It must not be used as a substitute for bank confirmations, asset title documents, custody records, independent valuations, or other evidence.

## 4. Distinction Between Project Claims and Verified Assets

For transparency, future MST disclosures should classify every proposed reserve item using the following categories:

### Category A — Project-Declared

An amount or asset description has been stated by the project or another interested party, but sufficient independent evidence has not yet been provided.

A project-declared figure is not a verified reserve.

### Category B — Documentary Evidence Received

Supporting documents have been received, but their authenticity, completeness, legal effect, ownership implications, or current validity have not yet been independently confirmed.

Receipt of a document alone does not establish that the related asset exists or is available.

### Category C — Independently Verified

The relevant asset has been examined by an appropriately qualified independent professional or institution.

The verification should address existence, ownership or control, valuation, relevant liabilities, restrictions, and the scope and date of the review.

Only assets that satisfy the applicable verification requirements should be described as independently verified.

### Category D — Eligible and Available for Backing

An asset has been verified and its legal availability for the stated backing arrangement has been established.

This assessment should consider ownership, liens, pledges, restrictions, competing claims, custody, enforceability, and any required legal arrangements.

Even this classification does not automatically establish a token holder's redemption right. Such a right requires separate legally valid documentation.

## 5. Evidence Required for Future Reserve Recognition

Before any asset is publicly described as verified MST backing, the project should obtain and review relevant evidence.

Depending on the asset type and jurisdiction, this may include:

### 5.1 Proof of Existence

Examples:

- Official bank confirmation.
- Custodian confirmation.
- Current account or asset statements.
- Independent physical inspection.
- Official registry extracts.
- Financial institution confirmations.
- Other reliable third-party evidence appropriate to the asset.

Documents should be current and independently authenticated where possible.

### 5.2 Proof of Ownership or Legal Control

Examples:

- Legal title documents.
- Official ownership registry records.
- Valid custody agreements.
- Executed purchase or transfer documents.
- Corporate ownership records.
- Security or pledge documentation.
- Legal opinions where appropriate.

The evidence must establish who owns or controls the asset and whether the relevant entity has the legal authority to use it for the proposed arrangement.

### 5.3 Independent Valuation

A valuation should identify:

- The asset being valued.
- The valuation date.
- The currency.
- The valuation method.
- The source of market data.
- Applicable assumptions.
- Any material limitations.
- The identity and qualifications of the valuation professional or institution.

A quoted exchange rate or online price alone is not an independent valuation of a reserve asset.

### 5.4 Liabilities and Restrictions

The reserve review should identify relevant:

- Loans and outstanding liabilities.
- Mortgages and liens.
- Pledges and security interests.
- Ownership disputes.
- Legal restrictions.
- Withdrawal or transfer restrictions.
- Third-party claims.
- Custody limitations.
- Taxes, fees, and other material obligations.

Gross asset value must not be presented as net available backing without considering relevant liabilities and restrictions.

### 5.5 Independent Verification

Where appropriate, an independent auditor, accounting firm, valuation professional, legal professional, custodian, or other qualified third party should verify the relevant information within a clearly defined scope.

The identity of the verifier, the date, the methodology, and the limitations of the verification should be disclosed where legally permissible.

A project statement or internally prepared spreadsheet is not a substitute for independent verification.

## 6. Reserve Register and Public Reporting

If actual reserve assets are established in the future, MST should maintain a reserve register.

Subject to privacy, confidentiality, and legal requirements, the public register should include:

| Field | Required Information |
|---|---|
| Reserve Reference | Unique asset identifier |
| Asset Category | Cash, financial instrument, property, commodity, or other |
| Currency | Applicable valuation currency |
| Declared Amount | Amount stated by the project |
| Verified Amount | Amount independently confirmed |
| Valuation Date | Date of valuation |
| Evidence Status | Declared, documents received, verified, or eligible |
| Ownership Status | Ownership or legal control assessment |
| Liability Status | Relevant liabilities and restrictions |
| Verification Provider | Independent verifier, if applicable |
| Report Reference | Public evidence or report identifier |
| Last Review Date | Date of most recent review |

The register should distinguish gross value from net available value.

Private bank account numbers, passwords, private keys, personal identification data, and other confidential security information must not be published in the public repository.

Confidential evidence may be reviewed through a secure and appropriate verification process without exposing sensitive account information publicly.

## 7. Currency Conversion and Exchange Rate Policy

If reserve assets are denominated in currencies different from the reporting currency, any conversion should identify:

- Original asset currency.
- Converted reporting currency.
- Exchange rate used.
- Source of the exchange rate.
- Date and time of the rate.
- Conversion methodology.

Indicative exchange rates from trading platforms or public market sources must not be described as proof of asset ownership or reserve existence.

Currency conversion changes the reported equivalent value; it does not create additional assets.

## 8. Reserve Custody and Availability

Where reserves are established, the project should disclose the custody arrangement to the extent legally and commercially permissible.

Relevant questions include:

- Which legal entity owns the asset?
- Which entity holds or safeguards it?
- Is the asset held in the project's name or a third party's name?
- Can the asset be transferred or withdrawn?
- Is it pledged or encumbered?
- Are there restrictions on access?
- Is the asset legally available for the stated purpose?
- Is there a documented mechanism connecting the asset to the token arrangement?

A statement that an asset exists does not automatically establish that it is controlled by the project or available to support MST.

## 9. No Automatic Redemption or Asset Ownership Rights

MST is an ERC-20 digital token.

Holding MST does not, by itself, establish:

- Ownership of a company.
- Ownership of real estate.
- Ownership of gold or other commodities.
- Ownership of a bank deposit.
- A beneficial interest in a reserve account.
- A right to demand payment.
- A right to redeem tokens for cash or assets.
- A claim against any particular reserve.

Any such rights would require separate, valid, applicable legal documentation and clearly defined contractual arrangements.

No redemption right should be inferred from the token name, contract constants, project descriptions, or future plans.

## 10. Future Reserve Development Policy

The project may explore the possibility of establishing documented reserve or collateral arrangements in the future.

Any such development is conditional and must not be interpreted as an existing reserve.

Before a reserve is recognized in public MST disclosures, the project should complete the relevant steps:

1. Identify the proposed asset.
2. Establish its existence.
3. Establish legal ownership or control.
4. Obtain appropriate supporting documents.
5. Determine relevant liabilities and restrictions.
6. Obtain an appropriate independent valuation.
7. Complete independent verification where applicable.
8. Establish the legal and custody arrangements.
9. Determine whether the asset is legally available for the stated backing purpose.
10. Publish a transparent reserve disclosure with evidence references and limitations.

No future reserve amount, verification date, or completion outcome is guaranteed by this document.

## 11. Reserve-to-Token Ratio

No verified reserve-to-token coverage ratio is claimed in this disclosure.

Such a ratio should only be published after both sides of the calculation have been established using reliable evidence.

A defensible calculation would need to identify:

- Net available verified reserve value.
- Total token supply at a stated block height and date.
- The valuation currency.
- Any relevant liabilities, restrictions, and exclusions.
- The calculation methodology.

A nominal contract constant must never be used as the verified reserve value in such a calculation.

A reserve ratio, even if independently calculated, does not automatically establish a guaranteed market price or redemption right.

## 12. Public Updates and Corrections

Material changes in reserve status should be reflected in the public documentation.

Updates should identify:

- The date of the change.
- The nature of the change.
- The evidence received.
- The verification status.
- The relevant public report or transaction reference, where available.
- Any limitations or unresolved issues.

Previous disclosures should not be silently rewritten in a way that hides material changes.

Where appropriate, the repository's commit history should provide a public record of documentation updates.
